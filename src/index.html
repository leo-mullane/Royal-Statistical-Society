Royal Statistical Society: website prototype

A clickable greyscale prototype of the whole new rss.org.uk, for agreeing structure, navigation and journeys before design. It covers the Must and Should requirements. Could requirements, including ClerksWell's suggestions, are not built as features: where a page shows the simplest form of one (for example a locked state instead of a redirect), its note is marked Proposed with the requirement number.

Open the home page

How to review

  • Annotations are off. Switch them on in the dark bar at the top of every page. Yellow notes explain behaviour and CMS capability; red notes are questions for RSS. Each note names the requirement it answers, e.g. REQ-201.

  • View as (dark bar) switches between signed out, a registered non-member, a member and a lapsed member, so you can see what changes for each.

  • Navigation, search, filters, the calendar, tabs, accordions, forms (with validation), the basket and the cookie banner all work. Individual article and profile links go nowhere by design.

  • Search is fully working; see "How search works" below. Try "Adaeze Okafor", "chartered statistician", "bayseian" (a typo) or "response rates" (found inside a PDF).

  • All people, employers, fees and figures are placeholders. Prices follow the viewer (member or non-member) and the date and number of delegates chosen, but the figures themselves are illustrative.

Navigation

  • Eight task-based sections replace the nine organisational ones: Membership, Professional development, Events and training, Get involved, Policy and research, News and publications, Resources, About.

  • The four use-based hubs from RSS's IA document (Professional development, Events and training, Get involved with volunteering, Resources) are top-level sections, not pages under Membership.

  • A utility bar links the RSS family of sites (Significance, Real World Data Science, RSS Publications), MyRSS, the basket and Join.

  • Every mega-menu panel links to its section's landing page, and two panels have search built in (events and experts).

Module library. The requirements trace is in requirements/requirements.md in the repository.

How search works

Built and working

  • One index across pages, events and courses, news, policy documents, sections and groups, experts and jobs. Every search box uses it, including the keyword boxes on the listing pages.

  • Ranking by page type (experts and groups, then pages, then events, jobs and news, then documents), with title, tag, summary and body weighted in that order. Every word has to match.

  • Suggestions as you type: topics, sections and groups first, then experts, then pages. Synonyms understood.

  • Synonyms (CStat and Chartered Statistician), spelling correction when nothing matches, highlighting, and text found inside PDFs, with the page it was on.

  • Tabs by type with counts; filters with live counts, chips and Clear all; filters at the side or across the top.

  • Everything in the address bar, so Back, Forward and copied links work. Section searches (events, experts) that widen to the whole site in one click.

  • Browse everything before typing; sort by relevance, date, year, type or title; experts by surname A to Z; a helpful no-results page; advanced search with a live count.

  • How search is configured shows that ranking, synonyms and filters are data, not code.

Later phase, not built

  • Promoted results for named searches (REQ-278, a Could). One example is built: search for "renew".

  • An editor-maintained synonym dictionary (REQ-047, a Could).

  • Search across Significance and Real World Data Science (REQ-291, a Could).

  • Search analytics dashboards: top and zero-result searches (REQ-118, shown as a CMS note).

  • Saved searches and alerts, Word and PowerPoint text, location-aware search for local groups, and matching reference numbers.

The AI summary (REQ-041) sits on top of the keyword search. All people, employers and documents in the search data are fictional.

Not in this draft

  • Could requirements, including ClerksWell's REQ-276 to REQ-291, and the Won't requirements. Where a Could would change a page, or a page shows its simplest form, a "Proposed" note says so.

  • Back-office and CMS functions (workflow, permissions, media library, newsletter editor, reporting). These are shown as CMS notes on the pages they affect.

  • The embedded conference programme (Phase 2 in the SoW).

  • Journeys for administrators, such as creating and sending newsletters and managing advertising. These are shown as CMS notes.

  3. v0.3: compact header switches without disappearing; payment added to joining, course booking, basket checkout, donations, renewal, invoices and job adverts, via a hosted payment page and a confirmation page.
  4. v0.4: menu and Events/Resources search boxes fixed; menu labels are buttons; separate qualification pages and Get involved pages; course date, price and delegates carry through booking and the basket; full date with waiting list; conference tickets priced by viewer; empty basket state; tab ARIA; Coulds labelled Proposed; requirement citations checked; consistency fixes.
  5. v0.5: search rebuilt to ClerksWell's search standard: ranking by page type, grouped suggestions, tabs with counts, filter layouts, copy link, section searches that widen, browse before typing, surname A to Z, advanced search builder with a live count, and a page showing how search is configured.
  6. v0.6: checked against RSS's key processes and user journeys. Added the accreditation application (with save and return and uploads) and an applications list in MyRSS; a short booking for free events; Payments and receipts in MyRSS; invoices for Direct Debit payments; two-step verification and other sign-in providers at sign-in; profile photo, specialisations and sign-in security in the profile; Sign out in the header.
